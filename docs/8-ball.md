# 8-ball pool: UK pub rules (reds and yellows)

Rules behind the frame tracker at `/games/pub-games/8-ball/`. The **primary ruleset is the English pub game** as most pubs play it: the old EPA pub rules (two visits after a foul, a free shot, cue ball in hand behind the baulk line). Where the governing bodies' rule books differ, that's noted inline and listed in full under [Rule sets and house rules](#rule-sets-and-house-rules).

Equipment: a 6 or 7 ft pub table with rounded pockets, 7 reds, 7 yellows, 1 black and a (usually slightly smaller) cue ball. Balls have no numbers. Reds and yellows are interchangeable: which colour is yours is decided during the frame.

## 1. Words used

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visit | One turn at the table. It lasts while you keep potting your own balls legally and ends on a miss or a foul. |
| Shot | One strike of the cue ball. A visit is one or more shots. |
| On | The balls you are allowed to hit first: your colour, the black once your colour is cleared, or any colour while the table is open. |
| Open table | Colours not yet decided. Either colour may be hit and potted. |
| Free shot | After a foul (pub and Blackball rules), the first shot of the incoming player's visit, where any ball may be hit first and any ball potted counts as legal. |
| Two visits | The usual foul penalty: the incoming player gets two turns in a row. |
| Baulk / baulk line | The line across the table a quarter of the way from the breaking end, and the area behind it. Ball in hand "in baulk" means place the cue ball anywhere on or behind that line. |
| In-off | The cue ball goes into a pocket. |
| Snookered | You can't hit any ball that is on in a straight line. Foul snookered = snookered as a result of the opponent's foul. |

## 2. Racking

- Rack with the triangle, apex towards the breaker, at the far end of the table.
- The **black sits in the middle of the third row, on the black spot**: the triangle is placed by the black, not by the front ball. This is the key difference from American pool, where the apex ball goes on the foot spot and the 8 just ends up in the middle.
- The usual pattern alternates colours so neither colour is bunched. Front ball is any colour; the two back corners are one red and one yellow. Most pub rules allow any pattern as long as the black is in the middle; the World Rules give a fixed diagram (reds and yellows alternating, with the two balls either side of the black in the third row being one of each).
- Balls must be touching. Nobody may re-rack the opponent's rack except to correct an obvious gap.
- Who breaks the first frame: toss a coin or lag. After that, **breaks alternate** frame by frame (some pubs: winner breaks, or loser breaks — agree it first).

## 3. The break

- Played from **anywhere behind the baulk line** (pub rules) or in the D (some older BAPTO/UKPF pub rules).
- **Legal break**: depends on the rule book.
  - Pub / old EPA: pot a ball, or at least **2 object balls reach a cushion**.
  - WEPF World Rules (1998–2022): pot a ball, or at least **4 object balls reach a cushion**.
  - WPA Blackball: pot a ball, or at least **2 object balls cross the middle-pocket line**.
  - International Rules (2022 on): score **3 points**: 1 per ball potted and 1 per ball fully past the middle-pocket line.
- **Illegal (foul) break**: the balls are re-racked and the opponent re-breaks with the foul penalty (two visits in pub/World rules). International Rules: re-rack and the opponent decides who breaks; no penalty visit.
- **Cue ball in-off on the break**: a foul in pub rules, so the opponent gets two visits with the cue ball in baulk. Under World Rules and International Rules it is only a loss of turn (one visit, cue ball in hand behind the baulk line). Blackball: two visits that don't carry over.
- **Black potted on the break**:
  - Pub, EPA, World Rules, Blackball: **no penalty, re-rack and the same player breaks again** — even if the cue ball or other balls also went down.
  - International Rules: the black is re-spotted. If it was the only ball potted the turn passes; if other balls went in on a legal break, the breaker carries on.
  - House variant: "black on the break wins" (common in American leagues and some bars) or, rarer, "black on the break loses".
- A ball potted on a legal break means the breaker keeps the table.

## 4. Open table and deciding colours

- After the break the table is **open**. You may hit either colour first and the black may not be hit first.
- **Colours are decided by the first ball legally potted after the break**: pot a red and you are reds for the rest of the frame, the opponent yellows. You carry on in that visit.
- **Balls potted on the break**: rule books disagree.
  - Pub / old EPA: the first ball legally potted, including on the break, decides. If both colours go down on one shot, the player nominates which colour they want.
  - World Rules: the breaker who pots must nominate a colour; if that colour went in on the break, it's theirs, otherwise the table stays open.
  - Blackball and International Rules: balls potted on the break don't decide colours; the breaker carries on with the table still open, and the next legal pot decides. (International: if a shot on the open table pots one of each colour, the colour of the ball nominated or hit first decides.)
- **Combination shots** (hitting one ball into another) are legal as long as the first ball hit is on.
- Balls potted on a foul while the table is open don't decide colours.

## 5. Playing the frame

- Every shot must **hit a ball that is on first**.
- After that first contact, **a ball must be potted or some ball (cue or object) must hit a cushion**. This "cushion rule" is in the World, Blackball and International rule books; old EPA pub rules don't require it, and many pubs don't play it.
- You keep shooting while you pot one of your own balls legally. A miss ends the visit; a foul ends it and gives the penalty.
- **Potting an opponent's ball**: a foul under pub/EPA and World Rules (except on a free shot). Under Blackball it's allowed if you also pot one of your own on the same shot. Under International Rules it's a loss of turn, not a foul. The ball stays down either way and counts for the owner.
- **Potting your own and an opponent's on one shot**: Blackball and International allow it (no penalty, you carry on). Pub/World: a foul unless it was a free shot.
- Once all your colours are down you are **on the black**: hit the black first on every shot.

## 6. Fouls

Any of these is a foul (standard penalty unless it's a loss-of-frame foul in section 8):

- **In-off**: the cue ball is potted.
- **Wrong ball first**: the cue ball first hits an opponent's ball, or the black before your colours are cleared (free shots excepted).
- **No contact**: the cue ball misses every ball.
- **No cushion after contact**, where the cushion rule is played (section 5).
- **Ball off the table**: any ball leaves the bed of the table. Object balls are re-spotted on the black spot (or as near as possible behind it); the cue ball is played from in hand.
- **Potting an opponent's ball** (pub and World rules, section 5).
- **Push shot or double hit**: the tip stays on the cue ball, or hits it twice. (World Rules allow a push with no double contact.)
- **Jump shot**: the cue ball jumps over any part of a ball before hitting the on ball.
- **Touching a ball**: touching any ball with the body, clothes, chalk or cue other than the tip striking the cue ball.
- **Both feet off the floor** when the shot is played (at least one foot must touch the floor).
- **Shooting before all balls have stopped**, or before re-spotted balls are placed.
- **Playing out of turn**.
- **Playing from outside baulk** when the cue ball is in hand and must be in baulk.
- **Failing to play away from a touching ball** correctly (section 9).
- **Time foul** in rules with a shot clock (section 11).

## 7. Penalty: two visits, free shot and ball in hand

**Pub / old EPA rules (primary):**

- The offender's visit ends and the **opponent gets two visits** in a row.
- The **first shot of the first visit is a free shot**: any ball may be hit first (including the black or the opponent's colour), and anything potted on it counts as legal (a potted black on a free shot loses, unless the player was already on the black, when it wins).
- If the cue ball is on the table, the incoming player may play from where it lies **or place it anywhere behind the baulk line** (in-hand) and play in any direction. Moving it does not count as a shot.
- **How the carry works**: pot a ball on the first visit and you simply carry on. When that first visit ends with a miss (not a foul), you still have the second visit, from where the cue ball lies. The free shot only applies to the very first shot. A foul during either visit loses whatever is left and the opponent gets two visits.

**Other rule books:**

- **World Rules**: two visits, **no free shot**, the cue ball stays where it is (in-hand in baulk only after an in-off or off the table). If the incoming player is foul snookered they may play a **free ball** (nominate any ball as on).
- **Blackball**: "**one shot, one visit**": a free shot, followed by one visit. If the free shot pots a ball you carry on in that visit; if it misses you get one more visit. Cue ball in hand behind the baulk line.
- **International Rules**: **one visit with ball in hand anywhere on the table**, no free shot.

**Two visits and the black**: under World and pub rules the two visits carry on to the black, so a player who clears their colours on the first visit can miss the black and still have the second visit at it. A common house rule removes this: **only one shot on the black** (the second visit is lost when you reach the black, or you get just one visit if you're on the black when the foul happens).

## 8. Winning and losing a frame

You **win** by legally potting the black after all your colours are down (in the nominated pocket, where the house plays "call the black").

You **lose** the frame if you:

- **Pot the black before your colours are cleared** (on any shot except the break). Example: black potted with yellows still on: you lose.
- **Pot the black with a foul on the same shot**, the classic being **black and in-off together**, but also black after hitting the wrong ball first.
- **Knock the black off the table**.
- Pot the black in a **pocket other than the one nominated**, where the black must be called.
- Pot your last colour and the black on the same shot (World and International rules; pub rules allow it on a free shot where only the black and opponent's balls are left).
- Commit a **deliberate foul** (pub, Blackball and International: loss of frame; World Rules: a standard foul), or clearly make no attempt to hit an on ball.
- In some pubs: go **in-off while on the black** even without potting it (house rule).

**Snookered on the black**: if you're on the black and can't hit it, you must still try; missing it is a foul and the opponent gets the penalty. If the opponent's foul has left you snookered on the black, World Rules give a free ball; pub rules give the free shot anyway.

**Stalemate**: if no legal shot is possible, or neither player can make progress, the frame is **re-racked and the original breaker breaks again** (the referee decides in league play).

## 9. Touching balls

- If the cue ball is **touching a ball that is on**, you may play away from it and that counts as hitting it; you must then still pot a ball or reach a cushion where the cushion rule applies. Moving the touching ball is not automatically a foul, but a push or double hit is.
- If it's **touching a ball that is not on** (opponent's or the black), you must play away and hit an on ball.
- International Rules: play away at 90 degrees or more from the line of the touching ball; when two of your own balls are touching the cue ball, play away from one.

## 10. Ball in hand and baulk detail

- In-hand in baulk: the cue ball's centre must be on or behind the baulk line (World 2015: a ball whose centre is exactly on the line is **not** in baulk). You may shoot in any direction.
- A foul committed before the cue ball has been placed (while in hand) still gives the opponent two visits with ball in hand.

## 11. Time limits

- Pub play: none, but don't stall. League play: usually a 45 s shot clock (International: 45 s per shot, one 15 s extension per frame; a time foul is a standard foul) or a shorter clock in finals.

## 12. American 8-ball (WPA) in brief

- Numbered balls: solids 1–7, stripes 9–15, the 8 in the middle of the rack with the **apex ball on the foot spot**.
- **Call shot**: every shot (WPA, BCA) or only the 8 (APA) is called by ball and pocket.
- Break: 4 balls to a rail or a ball potted. 8 on the break: re-spot or re-rack at the breaker's choice (WPA); win (APA, VNEA, most bars).
- **Every foul gives ball in hand anywhere on the table**, one visit. No two visits, no free shot, no baulk.
- The table is open after the break even if balls went in.
- Losing: 8 potted early, 8 in an uncalled pocket, scratch (in-off) or foul on the 8, 8 off the table.

## Rule sets and house rules

Every rule each ruleset changes, compared with the primary pub rules above. Where a row says "same", it matches the pub rule.

### WEPF World Rules (1998–2022, WEPF / EPA leagues)

- Break: pot a ball or **4 object balls to a cushion** (pub: 2).
- In-off on a fair break: loss of turn only, opponent plays from baulk (pub: two visits).
- Black on the break: re-rack, same breaker (same).
- Colours on the break: breaker nominates; potted colour nominated = theirs, otherwise open.
- **No free shot.** Two visits; the cue ball is played where it lies except after an in-off or off the table. **Free ball** when foul snookered.
- Cushion rule applies.
- Potting an opponent's ball: foul (same).
- Push shot allowed with no double contact; jump shots illegal.
- Two visits carry on to the black (same).
- Deliberate foul: standard foul, not loss of frame.
- Rolling up to a ball without hitting a cushion is a foul (cushion rule).

### International Rules (IEPF, formerly WEPF, and EPA from 2022)

- Break: **3 points** (1 per ball potted, 1 per ball past the middle-pocket line); short = re-rack and the opponent chooses who breaks.
- In-off on a legal break: loss of turn, one visit, cue ball in baulk.
- Black on the break: **re-spotted**; turn passes if it was the only ball potted.
- Colours: decided by the first ball potted after the break; balls on the break don't decide.
- Penalty: **one visit, ball in hand anywhere on the table**, no free shot.
- Potting an opponent's ball: **loss of turn**, not a foul. Own and opponent's in one shot: legal.
- Cushion rule applies (with a frozen-ball clause).
- Shot clock 45 s, one 15 s extension per frame.
- Deliberate foul, unsporting behaviour: loss of frame.
- Last colour and black on one shot: loss of frame.

### WPA Blackball (WPA, IPA; 2004 on)

- Break: pot a ball or **2 balls across the middle-pocket line**.
- In-off on the break: two visits that don't carry over.
- Black on the break: re-rack, same breaker (same).
- Colours: first ball legally potted after the break; break pots don't decide.
- Penalty: **free shot then one visit** ("one shot, one visit"), cue ball in hand in baulk.
- Potting an opponent's ball: **allowed when you also pot one of your own** on the same shot ("skill shot"); the black may go with your last colour on the same shot.
- Cushion rule applies. Push and jump shots illegal.
- Deliberate foul: loss of frame.

### English pub rules (old EPA pub rules, BAPTO/UKPF) — the primary rules

- Break: pot a ball or 2 to a cushion (BAPTO/UKPF later: 2 past halfway). BAPTO plays the break from the D.
- In-off on the break or foul break: re-rack, opponent breaks with two visits.
- First ball potted, including on the break, decides colours; both colours on one shot = player nominates. (BAPTO/UKPF: breaker nominates after the break.)
- Two visits, free shot first, cue ball in hand anywhere behind the baulk line (BAPTO: in the D).
- No cushion rule; rolling up to a ball is legal.
- Free shot: any ball may be potted. If only the black and opponent's balls remain, the black may be potted with them on the free shot.

### WPA American 8-ball (world standard)

- Numbered balls, apex on the foot spot, 8 in the centre. Groups are solids/stripes.
- Call every shot (obvious shots needn't be called); safeties may be declared.
- Break: 4 balls to a rail or a pot. Failing that is a foul; the opponent picks: take the table, re-rack and break, or have the breaker re-break.
- 8 on the break: breaker chooses re-spot or re-rack. 8 and scratch on the break: opponent chooses.
- Table open after the break regardless of pots.
- **Every foul: ball in hand anywhere**, one visit. Scratch on the break: ball in hand behind the head string (kitchen).
- Loss: 8 early, 8 in the wrong pocket, foul when potting the 8, 8 off the table.
- No three-foul rule in 8-ball (it's a 9-ball rule).

### BCA / CSI (BCAPL) league rules

- Like WPA, plus: call ball and pocket, no slop.
- Scratch on the break: ball in hand anywhere.
- 8 on the break: breaker chooses re-rack or re-spot (not a win).
- Scratching while shooting the 8: lose only if the 8 goes in; if the 8 doesn't drop it's a normal foul.
- Jump cues allowed.

### APA league rules

- **Slop counts** except on the 8 (no calling ordinary balls); the 8's pocket must be marked.
- 8 on the break: **win** (unless the cue ball also scratches: loss).
- Scratch on the break: opponent has ball in hand in the kitchen.
- A ball potted on the break commits you to that group if only one group went in.
- **Scratch while shooting the 8: loss** even if the 8 doesn't drop.
- Jump cues not allowed.

### VNEA league rules

- Must **mark the 8's pocket** (unless both agree beforehand).
- 8 on the break: **win**; the 8 off the table on the break: loss.
- Ball in hand anywhere after a foul; slop generally counts on object balls.
- Scratch on the 8 shot: loss.

### Common house rules (pubs and bars)

| House rule | What it changes | Tracker toggle |
|---|---|---|
| **Call the black** | Nominate the pocket before potting the black; black in another pocket loses | Yes |
| **Only one shot on the black** ("two shots don't carry on the black") | Reaching the black ends the second visit; a foul when the opponent is on the black gives only one visit | Yes ("Two visits carry on the black" off) |
| **Lose on an in-off while on the black** | An in-off when on the black loses the frame even if the black didn't go down | Yes |
| **Black and in-off loses** | Universal in every UK rule book | Built in |
| **Black on the break: re-rack / win / lose / re-spot** | Changes the black-on-break outcome | Yes |
| **Ball in hand anywhere** instead of two visits | International / American penalty | Yes (rule set) |
| **Three fouls in a row lose** | American 9-ball habit some bars apply to 8-ball | No |
| **Slop counts** | Fluked pots count; no calling ordinary balls (APA style; UK pubs never call ordinary balls anyway) | Built in (nothing is called) |
| **Call every shot** | Uncalled or fluked balls don't count | No |
| **Must hit a cushion** | Adds the cushion rule to pub rules | No (log it as a foul "no cushion") |
| **Loser / winner breaks** | Instead of alternating breaks | No (tracker alternates) |
| **Last pocket** (bar rule) | The 8 must go in the pocket where your last ball went | No (use "Call the black") |
| **Stalemate re-rack** | Original breaker breaks again | Built in ("Re-rack" button) |
