# 501 / 301 darts (x01): rules

Notes behind `/games/pub-games/501/`. "x01" is any game that counts down from a set score (301, 501, 701, 1001) to exactly zero.

## 1. Setup

- Board hung with the bull 1.73 m (5 ft 8 in) off the floor; the throwing line (oche) is 2.37 m (7 ft 9.25 in) from the face of the board measured along the floor. A foot may touch the oche but not go over it.
- 2 players or 2 teams in a match, but the scorer here also allows 1 (practice) and up to 8 (everyone plays for themselves).
- Standard start scores: 501 (pro and most leagues), 301 (shorter, common in pubs and with double-in), 701 and 1001 (long, team games). Any other number works the same way.
- A leg is one game from the start score to zero. A match is "first to" (or "best of" an odd number) of legs; formal play can group legs into sets.

## 2. Throwing order

- To decide who starts the first leg, each player throws one dart at the bull ("bull-off", "cork" in the UK pub vocabulary). The dart closest to the centre wins; it only counts if it stays in the board. Re-throw if both are in the same ring or the umpire cannot tell. Some leagues use a coin toss instead.
- Players then alternate visits ("turns") of up to three darts.
- The starter alternates every leg: the bull-off winner starts the odd legs, the other player the even legs. (Sets: the starter alternates per set and per leg, reversing in even sets.)
- If legs are level before a deciding leg, a bull-off may decide who throws first in it.
- All three darts of a visit are thrown before any are touched. Darts stay in the board until the score is called and written down.

## 3. Scoring

| Segment | Score |
|---|---|
| Single 1 to 20 (the large areas) | face value |
| Double ring (outer thin ring) | 2 x number (D1 = 2 up to D20 = 40) |
| Treble ring (inner thin ring) | 3 x number (T1 = 3 up to T20 = 60) |
| Outer bull (green ring) | 25 |
| Inner bull (red, "bullseye") | 50, and counts as a double (D25) |
| Off the board, bounce-out, in the wire but not stuck | 0 |

- There is no treble bull and no double outer bull.
- A dart scores where its point finishes when the visit's score is taken. If the point is in a wire, it scores for the side of the wire it entered.
- Maximum for a visit: 180 (three treble 20s). The highest checkout is 170 (T20, T20, bull).

## 4. Finishing (double-out)

- You must reach exactly zero, and the dart that takes you to zero must be a double (the inner bull counts).
- The first player to do that wins the leg. Any darts left in the visit are not thrown.
- You cannot finish on 1, because there is no double worth 1. You cannot finish on an odd number with one dart, and nobody can finish on 25 with one dart (no D12.5); you need two darts.
- Straight-out (no double needed) is common for beginners, kids and some pub leagues; see section 9.

## 5. Bust

A visit is a **bust** if, with the dart just thrown, you:

1. go below zero (score more than you need);
2. leave exactly 1 (double-out games), because 1 can never be finished;
3. reach zero without a double (double-out games).

When it happens:

- The whole visit is void. Your score goes back to what it was before your first dart of that visit, even if earlier darts in the visit were fine.
- The visit ends immediately; any remaining darts are not thrown.
- It still counts as your visit (you have used three darts for the average, in most scoring systems).
- Darts thrown after a bust or after a checkout are not thrown, and do not count. If an extra dart is thrown, it is ignored.
- Double-in (section 7): if the visit is a bust, the "doubled in" status goes back to what it was at the start of the visit too.

Example: you need 40 and throw T10 (30) then 10 (10)? That is zero but on a single: bust, you stay on 40. Another: you need 32, throw 20 then 11: that leaves 1: bust.

## 6. What can and cannot be finished

- Highest checkout in 3 darts with double-out is **170**. Any remaining score above 170 cannot be finished in one visit.
- **Bogey numbers** (not finishable in 3 darts on double-out, so you cannot finish at all from them in one visit): **169, 168, 166, 165, 163, 162, 159**. Anything from 171 upwards also cannot be finished in one visit.
- Every other score from 2 to 170 can be finished in three darts or fewer (160, 161, 164, 167 and 170 are finishable, but only with treble-heavy routes).
- Remaining scores that are one-dart finishes: even numbers 2 to 40, and 50.
- **Impossible 3-dart visit totals** (no combination of three darts makes them): **163, 166, 169, 172, 173, 175, 176, 178, 179**, and anything over 180. (181+ is impossible; 180 is possible.)
- The scorer here rejects an impossible visit total (such as 179 or 181), and rejects a total that would reach zero when no legal finish exists (for instance "3 on a double with that").

## 7. Double-in (and master-in)

- **Straight-in** (the default in PDC/WDF 501): any dart scores from the first dart.
- **Double-in**: you score nothing until a dart lands in a double (or inner bull). Darts before that count as 0. Common with 301. The first scoring dart is the double itself, so you start at 301 minus that double (e.g. D16 takes 301 to 269).
- **Master-in**: a double or a treble (or inner bull) opens the scoring.
- After you are in, you stay in for the rest of the leg (a bust returns you to the status at the start of that visit).

## 8. Awkward cases

- **Bounce-out / dart falls out:** scores zero, counts as one of your three darts, is not re-thrown. If it falls out after the score was taken, the score stands.
- **Dart sticks in another dart (Robin Hood):** the dart that lands in the other one is not "in the board" and is normally a no-score ("dead dart"); some leagues allow a re-throw. If it knocks another dart out of a treble, the knocked dart counts for where it finally lands. (Not covered by the WDF text we found; check your league.)
- **Dart hitting the wire:** scores from the side of the wire it entered; if it does not stick, zero.
- **Dart dropped before a clear throw:** can be picked up and thrown again (it was never thrown). Once thrown it counts.
- **Touching darts in the board during your throw:** the throw is considered complete.
- **Throwing out of turn:** the dart(s) are void and the right player carries on. The WDF text we found only says the referee must make sure the order is followed; the "void and re-throw" penalty is the common league convention.
- **Wrong score taken:** arithmetic mistakes stand unless corrected before the next visit.
- **Time:** about 5 minutes to be at the board when called, 6 practice darts before a match.

## 9. Rule sets and house rules

What each common rule set changes versus the "standard" described above (PDC/WDF 501, straight-in, double-out, three-dart visits, bust voids the visit).

| Rule set | Start score | In | Out | Bull | Legs / sets | What differs from standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PDC** (Professional Darts Corporation) | 501; 301 in some events | Straight-in. The World Grand Prix is the long-standing exception: double-in and double-out. | Double-out (inner bull counts) | Outer 25, inner 50 | Mostly legs (match play), sets in a few events; sudden-death / bull decider when tied in some | Darts removed after each throw only once the score is agreed; modern events add a strict raised oche and referee calls. |
| **WDF / BDO (Lakeside rules)** | 501 | Straight-in | Double-out | Outer 25, inner 50 | Legs and sets (best of odd numbers); starter alternates per leg, reversing in even sets | The written rulebook is the reference for bust, bounce-outs (no re-throw) and bull-off. |
| **ADO / NDA / league and pub rules** (US-style leagues, local pub leagues) | 301 or 501 (301 commonly) | Often double-in for 301, sometimes straight-in | Double-out, or master-out, or straight-out | Often 25 and 50, but some leagues have the full bull as double-only | Fixed leg counts, team points per leg | Local rules on dart in a dart, re-throws for bounce-outs, "dead darts"; often a 3-dart visit with an "out" fixed by the league. Check with your league. |
| **Casual / house rules** | 301, 501, 701 | Straight-in or double-in | Double-out, straight-out (for kids and beginners), or master-out | See bull options below | First to N legs, or one game | See list below. |

Common house rules and variants, and what each changes:

- **Straight-out:** no double needed to finish. Bust only when you go below zero (leaving 1 is fine). Easier; checkouts such as 3 and 25 become possible and any single dart can end the game.
- **Master-out:** the finishing dart must be a double or a treble (or inner bull). Leaving 1 is still a bust.
- **Double-in / master-in:** see section 7. Often used with 301.
- **Straight-in (default):** all darts count from the start.
- **50/50 bull ("fat bull"):** outer and inner bull both score 50, only the inner bull counts as a double. Some pubs use it; leagues do not. (Split bull is the standard: 25 and 50.)
- **Bounce-outs re-thrown:** a dart that bounces out is thrown again (not standard; the dart is dead in the WDF text).
- **Dart in a dart re-thrown:** allowed in some pubs.
- **Bull-off vs coin toss vs "loser starts":** how the first leg is decided; and whether the loser or the winner of a leg throws first next (standard: alternate).
- **Win by two legs / sudden death:** some leagues play on until one side is two legs ahead; others settle a tie with a bull-off for the throw in a deciding leg.
- **Bust handling:** standard is that the visit is void. A few casual groups let you keep the score of the darts before the bust (not recommended), or allow you to go "below zero and back" (no).
- **Handicaps:** weaker players start from a higher or lower score or must double-in only; a start score of 501 for the strong and 301 for the weak.
- **Round limits:** a maximum number of visits, lowest score wins.
- **Closest-to-zero tiebreak:** when a time limit ends the game, the lowest remaining score wins.
- **Sets:** groups of legs; this page only supports "first to N legs" to keep it simple.

The page offers: start score (301, 501, 701 or custom), in (straight, double, master), out (double, master, straight), bull (split 25/50, or 50/50) and first to N legs. Everything else on this list is a convention to agree with your opponent before the game.
