# Killer darts: rules

Killer is an elimination game for three or more players (two can play, but it is short). Each player owns one number on the board. You earn the right to attack by hitting your own double, then you hit other players' doubles to take their lives. The last player with a life left wins.

## Numbers

- Each player throws one dart with their weaker hand (the left hand for a right-hander). The number the dart lands in is that player's number.
- If two players land on the same number, the later player throws again. A miss is also thrown again.
- Variants: numbers are drawn at random (from a hat, "Blind Killer", where nobody knows who owns which number until they are hit), or players simply choose a free number. This site's tracker can assign random unique numbers from 1 to 20 or take numbers you type in, and it refuses duplicates.
- The bull is nobody's number in the standard game.

## Lives

- Everyone starts with 3 lives. 5 lives is the common longer game.

## Becoming a killer

- On your turn you throw three darts at your own double. One dart in your double makes you a killer (marked with a K on the scoreboard).
- Variants:
  - Needing two or three hits on your own double before you become a killer (slower, fairer for strong players).
  - Trebles of your own number count as a hit, or as two hits.
  - Killer Cricket: you must close your number (three marks) before you can become a killer.
- Until you are a killer, hitting anyone else's double does nothing.

## Killing

- A killer throws at other players' doubles. Each double hit takes one life from the owner.
- Variants: a treble takes two lives (or three); or only doubles count and trebles are ignored (the standard rule).
- Anyone can be hit, killer or not. A player who is not yet a killer still loses lives.
- A player with no lives left is eliminated, and their number is dead: hitting it does nothing.
- Singles never count for anything. The bull is nobody's number, so it does nothing (and there is no treble bull).

## Hitting your own double once you are a killer

Sources disagree, so agree this before you start. Common versions:

1. You lose one life (the most common house rule).
2. You lose your killer status and must hit your own double again to get it back.
3. Both: you lose a life and your killer status.
4. A scaled penalty: a double costs 2 lives and a treble costs 3.

You can hit your own double by accident when aiming at a neighbouring number, which is the reason the rule exists. This site's tracker offers versions 1 and 2 and "nothing happens".

## Losing killer status

- In some variants a killer who loses a life (is hit by another killer) also loses their killer status and has to earn it again.
- In the standard game you stay a killer until you are eliminated, apart from the self-hit rule above.

## Turns

- Each turn is three darts, in a fixed order. Eliminated players are skipped.
- All three darts can be used to become a killer and then to kill, in the same turn.

## Winning

- The last player with at least one life wins. Play stops as soon as only one player is left.
- Darts are thrown one at a time, so two players cannot be eliminated by the same dart in the normal game. If a killer knocks themselves out with a self-hit penalty, they are out and the remaining player or players carry on; if that leaves one player, that player wins.
- With a "two lives" treble, a player on one life is simply eliminated (lives never go below zero).

## Rule sets and house rules

"Standard" below means: numbers by weak-hand throw, 3 lives, one double of your own number makes you a killer, only doubles count, a double on a rival takes 1 life, a killer's own double costs a life (or killer status, see above), last player standing wins. Every row lists only what it changes. Items marked (site) are toggles in the tracker's setup; the others are documented but not built.

### Number assignment

| Method | Changes |
|---|---|
| Weak-hand throw (standard) | One dart each with the non-dominant hand; duplicates re-thrown. Avoid the bull as a number for casual groups. |
| Random draw / Blind Killer | Numbers drawn from a hat or written down and hidden. Players only learn an owner when the number is hit. Some versions start everyone as a killer with full lives and eliminate a player after their number is hit three times. |
| Chosen numbers | Players pick any free number. Tip: avoid sitting next to a rival's number, since a miss can hit their double. (site: random unique numbers or typed numbers, duplicates refused) |
| Bull as a number | The bull (outer bull counts) can be someone's number. Not offered on the site. |

### Lives

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| 3 lives (standard) | |
| 5 or 6 lives | Longer game. (site: 1 to 9 lives) |
| Handicap | Stronger players need a treble (or three hits) to become a killer, or start with fewer lives. (site: hits needed 1 to 3) |
| Gain lives by hitting your own number | Gran Darts style: your own number gives lives (single +1, double +2, treble +3) and you become a killer on reaching 3; rivals' hits take the same amounts off. (site: "Any part of the number" counts plus the "gain lives" self-hit option approximates this) |

### Which darts count

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| Doubles only (standard) | Only the double ring of a number counts, worth 1. (site) |
| Doubles and trebles | A treble is worth 2 (some groups 3). (site: treble = 2) |
| Trebles only | The treble ring replaces the double ring. (site) |
| Any part of the number | Single = 1, double = 2, treble = 3. Fast, bloody games. (site) |
| Hits needed | Hit your own double 2 or 3 times (often non-consecutive) before becoming a killer; or a points target (single 1, double 2, treble 3, 5 points to qualify). (site: hits needed 1 to 3, counted with the dart weights above) |

### Killer status

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| Earn it (standard) | Everyone starts as a non-killer. |
| Killer Straight Off | Everyone starts as a killer. (site) |
| Lose status when hit | A killer who loses a life to an attack reverts to non-killer and must earn killer status again; one source also makes them win their lives back by hitting their own number. (site: status only) |
| One killer at a time | Others become a killer by hitting the current killer's double. Not built. |

### Self-hit penalty (a killer hits their own number)

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| Lose one life | The most common rule. (site) |
| Lose lives equal to the dart | Double 2, treble 3 (or by the dart's weight). (site) |
| Lose killer status | Must re-earn it with your own double. (site) |
| Lose a life and status | Both penalties. (site) |
| Gain lives | The hit restores lives up to the starting number (Gran Darts "life recovery"). (site) |
| Nothing | Harmless. (site) |

### Bull

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| Dead (standard) | The bull is nobody's number and does nothing; no treble bull exists. (site) |
| Bull gives a life | Any bull (outer or inner) gives the thrower an extra life, up to the starting number (reader variant on DartHelp). (site) |
| Bull as a number | See number assignment. |

### Other

| Variant | Changes |
|---|---|
| Resurrection | Eliminated players return after hitting a set target. Not built. |
| Teams | Pairs or trios; teammates cannot hit each other; last team standing wins. Not built. |
| Killer Cricket | Close your number (three marks) before becoming a killer. Not built. |
| Numbers 10 to 20 plus bull, closing numbers | A scoring game borrowed from Cricket; not really Killer. Not built. |
