# 9-ball pool: rules

Primary rule set: **WPA World Standardized Rules** (the WPA Rules of Play, used by most pro events). Bar and league variants follow, with every change from the WPA rules listed. The rack tracker at `/games/pub-games/9-ball/` implements the WPA rules and the toggles marked **[toggle]** below.

Confidence note: the WPA points come from the WPA rules and standard published summaries. Matchroom/Mosconi, APA, BCA and VNEA details come from secondary summaries and forum reports, and leagues revise their books every year, so check the book you are playing under.

## 1. The game

- Played with the cue ball and object balls 1 to 9. Each shot must first hit the **lowest-numbered ball on the table** (the "ball on").
- Once that contact is legal, any ball may be potted. The rack is won by legally potting the **9**.
- Matches are a **race to N racks**.

## 2. Racking

- Diamond rack, nine balls. The **1 is at the apex** on the foot spot, the **9 is in the centre**, the other balls in random order, all touching. Use a template rack where possible.
- The breaker shoots from behind the head string (the "kitchen").

## 3. The break

- Cue ball in hand behind the head string. The cue ball must **hit the 1 first**.
- A legal break needs a ball **potted**, or **at least four object balls driven to a rail**. Otherwise it is an illegal break, which is a foul.
- Balls potted on a legal break stay down (except a 9 that is spotted after a foul). The breaker **keeps shooting** if any ball is potted. If none is, the turn passes with no ball in hand.
- **The 9 potted on a legal break wins the rack.**
- **Break fouls** (scratch, 1 not hit first, illegal break, ball jumped off the table): the incoming player gets **ball in hand**. Under WPA rules the incoming player may, after an illegal break, choose to have the balls re-racked instead (this tracker simply gives ball in hand). Balls potted on a foul break stay down. A 9 potted on a foul break is **spotted** and does not win.
- Who breaks: **alternate breaks** (WPA standard) or **winner breaks** (common in leagues and bars). **[toggle]**

## 4. Push-out

- Only on the **shot immediately after the break shot**.
- The player must **announce** the push-out to the opponent (and referee) before shooting. Otherwise it is a normal shot.
- On a push-out the cue ball need not hit any ball or rail, and balls potted stay down, except the **9, which is spotted**. Potting a ball does not let the pusher keep the turn.
- The opponent then **chooses**: shoot from where the balls stopped, or **pass the shot back** to the pusher.
- A push-out that also fouls (for example a scratch) is a foul as usual. **[toggle: push-out allowed]**

## 5. A legal shot

After the break, a shot is legal when the cue ball:

1. hits the **lowest-numbered ball** on the table first, and
2. then a ball is potted, or any ball (cue or object) touches a rail.

Balls potted on a legal shot stay down and the shooter keeps going. If nothing is potted, the turn passes (no ball in hand).

- **Combination and carom shots on the 9** are legal and win: hit the lowest ball and drive the 9 into a pocket, directly or via other balls.
- In standard WPA play you do not call shots. A 9 potted on a legal shot wins, even by luck ("slop"). **[toggle: call the 9]**

## 6. Fouls

- **Scratch**: cue ball potted.
- **Wrong ball first**: the cue ball contacts anything other than the lowest-numbered ball first.
- **No rail after contact**: nothing potted and no ball reaches a rail after the legal contact.
- **Jumped off the table**: cue ball or any object ball ends up off the table.
- **Push / double hit**: pushing the cue ball through, or hitting it twice on one stroke.
- **Touching a ball** with hand, clothing or cue (other than the tip on the stroke).
- **Feet**: at least one foot must be on the floor at the stroke (some bar wordings say "both feet off the floor" is the foul).
- Shooting out of turn, illegal push-out, shooting while balls move, and so on.

Consequences:

- The opponent gets **ball in hand anywhere on the table**. **[toggle: behind the head string]**
- Balls potted on a foul **stay down**, except the **9, which is spotted** on the foot spot (or as near as possible along the long string).
- **Jumped object balls** stay off the table, except the **9, which is spotted**.
- The fouling player's turn ends.

### Three consecutive fouls

A player who commits **three fouls on three consecutive shots of their own** (no legal shot between; the opponent's shots do not reset it) **loses the rack**. The player must be **warned after the second foul**. A legal shot resets the count. **[toggle: three-foul rule]**

## 7. Winning and spotting the 9

- Legal 9, whether on the break, straight, in a combination or by carom: **win**.
- 9 potted on a **foul**: **spotted**, not a win. The incoming player has ball in hand.
- **Stalemate**: if the referee and both players agree there is no progress, the balls are re-racked and the same player breaks again.

## 8. 10-ball (brief)

10-ball uses balls 1 to 10 in a **triangle rack**, the 1 at the apex and the 10 in the middle. You must **call the ball and pocket** on every shot except the break. A ball potted without being called stays down (the 10 is spotted) and the turn ends, but this is not a foul by itself. A 10 on the break does not win and is spotted. Push-outs, ball in hand and the three-foul rule work as in 9-ball.

## 9. Rule sets and house rules

Everything below is a **change from the WPA rules above**.

### 9.1 WPA World Standardized Rules (baseline)

Alternate breaks, 9 on the break wins, push-out on the shot after the break, ball in hand anywhere, three-foul rule, no calling, 9 spotted on fouls.

### 9.2 Matchroom / Mosconi Cup, Predator / World Nineball Tour (pro)

These use the WPA rules with the following differences (reported by secondary sources; confirm against the event's official rules):

- **Break box**: the break is taken from inside a marked box behind the head string, not anywhere behind it.
- **3-point rule**: a break is legal only if **at least three object balls are potted or cross the head string**; a weaker break is a foul or lets the opponent take the table or have the breaker re-break. Sources disagree on whether the Mosconi Cup uses it.
- **9 on the spot** racks: the 9 sits on the foot spot with the 1 at the apex, so the corner balls are not automatic (as reported for Matchroom events).
- **Shot clock**: commonly 30 seconds per shot with a limited number of extensions per rack. Running out is a foul. (Not confirmed in the sources found.)
- Push-out, ball in hand and the three-foul rule as WPA. In Mosconi Cup team format the order of players is set by captains.

### 9.3 League rule sets

- **APA**: 9-ball is scored by handicap and points per ball, not simply by racks. A foul on a legal break gives **ball in hand anywhere**. The 9 on the break wins, but a **scratch on the break spots the 9** and passes the turn. Shots are not called. The three-foul rule differs from WPA, so check the current book.
- **BCA league**: close to WPA, with call-shot emphasis in some formats. The break is taken with the cue ball in hand behind the head string. Three-foul rule not used everywhere.
- **VNEA**: best known for 8-ball. Its 9-ball format follows BCA-style rules; check the current book.

### 9.4 Bar rules (coin-operated tables)

- **Slop counts**: any 9 potted on a legal hit wins, even by luck.
- **No push-out** (often not known). **[toggle]**
- **Ball in hand behind the head string** after a scratch. **[toggle]**
- **Winner breaks.** **[toggle]**
- Often **no three-foul rule**. **[toggle]**
- **Cue-ball fouls only**: only a scratch or the cue ball leaving the table is a foul. **[toggle]**
- **Call the 9**, and a 9 on the break may not win. **[toggle]**

### 9.5 Common house rules

| House rule | What it changes vs WPA | In the tracker |
|---|---|---|
| Golden break (9 on the break wins) | WPA already awards the win; some bars do not (see call the 9) | 9 on the break: wins |
| Call the 9 | The 9 must be called (or be the last ball) to win. An uncalled 9 is spotted and the turn ends, also on the break | "Call the 9" and "9 on the break: spotted" |
| Two-way shot | A deliberate safety that could also pot a ball. Fine under slop rules; with call-shot rules the shooter must say "safe" | Not tracked |
| Ball in hand behind the line | After a foul the cue ball goes behind the head string, not anywhere | Toggle |
| Cue-ball fouls only | Only scratches and cue ball off the table are fouls | Toggle |
| Slop | No calling; any legal pot counts, including luck | Default |
| Alternate or winner breaks | WPA alternates; bars often let the winner break | Toggle |
| No push-out | Removes the push-out | Toggle |
| Early 9 penalty | Calling the 9 early and missing lets the opponent take the shot or pass back | Not tracked |
