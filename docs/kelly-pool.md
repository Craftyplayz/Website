# Kelly Pool: full rules

Kelly Pool (also called pea pool or pill pool) is a rotation game for two or more players. Each player secretly draws a numbered pea. The aim is to pot the ball with your number, or, in the elimination versions, to be the last player whose number is still alive.

There is no single official rule set. The Billiard Congress of America (BCA) lists two variants (a simple one and a points one); pubs and leagues add their own. This document gives the standard rules first, then every common alternative with what it changes. Where sources disagree, both are given. The tracker on `/games/kelly-pool/` follows the choices marked **Tracker**.

## 1. Equipment

- A pool table, the cue ball and the 15 object balls (1 to 15).
- 15 numbered **peas** (also called pills or tally balls), small numbered markers that fit a narrow-necked **shake bottle**. A 16th pea for the cue ball is optional (see 11).
- No peas? Numbered bottle caps in a cup work. Another way is to cover the balls with a cloth, let each player pick one blindly, note which, and put it back. The tracker replaces all of this: it deals the numbers on a phone.
- Optional: a bottle or pot on the table for the stake (see 9).

## 2. Players and drawing peas

- Two players is the minimum. Four to six is the usual best size, and up to 15 can play (one pea each).
- Shake the bottle and have each player draw one pea. The number is their **private number**. Look at it, then close your hand and keep it secret.
- Seat order or draw order sets the shooting order. Many tables let the player who drew the lowest pea shoot first; others use a lag or simply start left of the racker.
- **Extra peas.** With fewer than 15 players, some numbers are not drawn. Those balls belong to nobody ("dead balls" or "orphans"). Potting one scores nothing and does not end the game.
- **Several peas each.** A common variant deals two or more peas per player (the tracker allows 1 to 5, as long as players x balls is 15 or fewer). The player is then out only when all their numbers are gone, or wins when any one of them is legally potted, depending on mode (see 11).

## 3. Rack, break and order of play

- Rack all 15 balls. The Wikipedia entry gives the 1 ball at the apex on the foot spot, the 2 and 3 in the back corners, and the rest random.
- The sources give no formal break rule. Treat the break as the first shot: it counts as a normal shot and the first object ball hit is the 1. Common practice is that the breaker must hit the 1 first (it is at the apex) and a ball potted on the break counts as a normal pot.
- Play goes in rotation round the table. After a legal pot of the lowest ball the shooter continues; otherwise the turn passes.

## 4. Legal shots and the lowest-ball rule

- **Rotation (standard).** On every shot the cue ball must first touch the **lowest-numbered ball still on the table**, whoever it belongs to. This is the same first-contact rule as 9-ball.
- **No safeties and no calling.** You don't call shots or pockets. Any ball that drops on a legal shot counts, including by combination or carom, unless noted below.
- **Legal pot.** A ball counts when it drops in a pocket on a shot that is not a foul. Your own number only wins if the shot had no foul on it (Wikipedia).
- **Continuing.** If you legally pot a ball you shoot again. Some tables only let you continue after potting the lowest ball; most let you continue after any legal pot.
- **Any ball (call-shot style).** In the childhood-style "pea pool" version players may shoot any ball. This changes the game a lot because players can go straight for their own number.

## 5. Potting other players' balls

This is where the rule sets differ most.

- **Standard (simple BCA version).** Potting another player's number does nothing except remove that ball from the table. Only potting your own number wins. Because players don't know whose ball it is, the owner usually stays silent.
- **Kill / dead.** In the common elimination style, when someone else pots your number you are **dead** (killed): you cannot now win with that number. A dead player keeps shooting in rotation (to hit balls and to avoid the table going quiet) but cannot win. You must announce that you are dead before your next shot (see 8).
- **Out of the round.** Another version has the owner leave the game for that round.
- **Points (BCA scoring variant).** Pot another's number and you score 1 point; the owner loses 1 point. See 11.
- **Last man standing.** Every ball potted by anyone, including the owner, costs its owner a life. Last player alive wins. See 11.
- **Potting two players' balls in one shot.** Both owners are affected by whichever rule is in play. In elimination styles both are dead, and in points styles the shooter scores for each. If that would leave nobody alive, see 10.

## 6. Fouls and penalties

Fouls are not standardised. These are the common ones:

- Cue ball pocketed (scratch).
- Cue ball off the table.
- Wrong ball hit first (not the lowest on the table), or no ball hit.
- No ball driven to a rail after contact (some tables only).
- Touching a ball with your hand, cue or clothing, or a double hit.
- Knocking over the shake bottle or the stake pot (some tables: a small fine).

Penalties, from lightest to heaviest:

- **No penalty beyond the foul (Wikipedia/BCA).** The incoming player may play the table as it lies or make the fouler shoot again.
- **Cue ball in hand.** After a scratch or the cue ball leaving the table, the incoming player gets the cue ball in hand behind the head string (kitchen). Some tables give ball in hand anywhere after any foul.
- **Turn ends.** The shooter's turn ends and the next player shoots from where the balls lie.
- **Pay into the pot.** The fouler pays a set amount (for example 25 cents to $1) into the pot or to the other players. Common for scratches and knocking the bottle.
- **Re-spot.** Any ball that drops on a foul does not count. It goes back on the table (see below), and the owner is not killed.
- **A ball potted on a foul and the 15 rule.** One house rule bans combinations on the 15 unless the 15 is the shooter's own number; a combo on the 15 is spotted.

### Spotting balls

Illegally potted balls are spotted on the foot spot, or as close as possible behind it toward the foot rail if that spot is taken. Several balls spot in numerical order, lowest closest to the head of the table. If the cue ball is the thing that dropped, it comes back by the ball-in-hand rule above.

## 7. Winning

- **Simple version.** The first player to legally pot their own number wins.
- **Last man standing / kill.** When every other player is dead or out, the one left wins. If your own number is potted by you, in this style you still lose a life.
- **Everyone out at once.** If a single shot would eliminate every remaining player, the usual ruling is that the shooter, if still alive, stays alive; otherwise re-rack and play again.
- **No winner.** If all balls are potted and nobody has won (common in dead-player games), re-rack, redraw, and replay. Many tables double the stake on the replay (a "rollover" or "double").
- **The 15 as the end ball.** In one version, if nobody has made their number, whoever pots the 15 wins.

## 8. Revealing peas, peeking and cheating

- **Peeking.** Each player may look at their own pea at any time. Nobody may look at another's. Looking at someone else's pea or tipping your hand with your behaviour is a forfeit in most houses.
- **Announcing death.** If your number is potted by someone else you must say so before your next shot. Failing to announce is a penalty: in the strictest rule sets you forfeit and the potter scores a double point; other tables fine you $1.
- **Showing a pea.** A potted ball's owner is revealed when the ball drops (if the owner is dead). A player who pots their own number shows their pea to prove the win.
- **Disputes.** Any player may ask a winner to show the pea. A mismatch loses the game for the claimant.
- **Throwing the game.** Colluding, steering balls to help a partner or deliberately hitting the wrong ball to pass the turn is cheating.

## 9. Money and stakes

- **Ante and pot.** Each player puts an agreed amount (for example 25 cents or $1) in a pot. The winner takes it.
- **Per kill.** Another style pays a set amount (for example 25 cents) to whoever pots another's number, paid by the owner.
- **Winner paid by all.** The winner collects a fixed amount from every player, often double the kill value.
- **Rollover.** If no one wins, the next game pays double.
- **Groups.** At bigger venues groups of five play winner-take-all, and the group winners then play each other.
- Fines (scratch, bottle knocked, forgot to announce) go in the pot or to the nearest player, as the house chooses.

## 10. Edge cases

| Situation | Usual ruling |
|---|---|
| You pot your own ball on a foul | It does not count. Spot the ball. You are not the winner and the ball stays live. |
| Two players' balls drop on one shot | Both are affected. With a points rule the shooter scores for each. |
| Your ball is potted by someone else when you're already out | Nothing changes. The ball is just removed. |
| Ball 15 (or any ball) potted very early | Treated like any ball. If nobody held it, nothing happens. If someone did, they are dead or lose a life. |
| A player has no live ball left | They are out (or dead) and keep shooting only if the table plays on in rotation with dead players. |
| All remaining live balls are potted in one shot | Shooter is alive: they win if their own ball dropped legally. If that is not the case, re-rack. |
| Your own ball dropped by a legal shot of an opponent | Rule-set specific: you are dead (kill), lose a life (lms), or nothing (simple). |
| Cue ball drops with an object ball | Foul. The object ball is spotted. |
| Peas run out or a pea is lost | Redraw all peas or use numbered caps. Don't continue with a missing pea. |
| A player leaves mid-game | Treat their number as a dead ball and play on. |

## 11. Rule sets and house rules

| Rule set / house rule | What it changes against the standard rules |
|---|---|
| **Simple BCA** | First to pot their own number wins. Other players' numbers are just removed from the table. |
| **BCA points** | Winner is paid 2 points by each player. Potting another's number gives you 1 point and the owner loses 1. The owner stays in the game. Failing to announce a killed number forfeits that player and the potter gets 2 points. If no one pots their own number the game ends when the last number drops and is replayed at double points. |
| **Kill / dead players** | Owner is dead when another player pots their number, keeps shooting in rotation, and must announce it. A dead player cannot win. |
| **Out of the round** | A killed player leaves the game and stops shooting. |
| **Last man standing** | Each number is a life. A potted ball costs its owner a life whoever sank it, including the owner. Last player with a life wins. Potting your own number does not win. **Tracker: the only mode.** |
| **Lives / several peas** | Each player holds 2 to 5 numbers. Out only when all are gone (lms), or win when any of your own is potted (simple). **Tracker: 1 to 5 balls each, last man standing only.** |
| **Rotation vs call-shot** | Rotation: must hit the lowest ball first. Call-shot or any-ball: no lowest-ball rule, which favours players who can go straight for their number. **Tracker: rotation text only.** |
| **Kill scoring / payouts per ball** | Money moves on every kill (for example 25 cents from the owner to the shooter) and on the win (from every player). |
| **Ante pot** | One pot, winner takes all, no per-kill payments. |
| **Extra pills / ghost pea** | Draw more pills than players (up to all 15). Unclaimed numbers are dead balls and potting them does nothing. With a 16 pea, the cue ball is a private number: the holder wins by hitting the lowest ball and scratching, or is dead if another player scratches after hitting the lowest ball first. |
| **15 as end ball** | If nobody pots their number, potting the 15 wins. Combos on the 15 allowed only if it is your number. |
| **Smaller rack** | With 4 or 5 players use only 6 or 9 balls so more of them are live. Drop the cue-ball pea. |
| **Rollover / double** | A game with no winner is replayed for double the stake. |
| **Fines** | Scratch, knocked bottle or unannounced death costs a set amount. |
| **Ball in hand after any foul** | A stricter foul rule than the usual "ball in hand after scratch only". |
| **Winner reveal** | Winner must show their pea. A false claim loses. |

## 12. What the tracker does

The tracker on `/games/kelly-pool/` replaces the peas and bottle and keeps count of lives. It does not track the cue ball, fouls, money or the shooting order (turn tracking is optional).

- **Mode:** last man standing only. Potting your own ball never wins; leave your balls on the table.
- **Balls:** 2 to 15 players, 1 to 5 balls each, players x balls at most 15. Unused balls belong to no one.
- **Last man standing:** whoever pots it, the owner loses one life. Last player alive wins.
- **Not tracked:** fouls, scratches, penalties and money. If a ball is potted on a foul, don't tap it (or use Undo).
- **No winner:** if every ball is potted with no winner the game ends as no winner.
- **Privacy:** numbers show only after a pass screen, and everyone's numbers are shown once the game ends.
