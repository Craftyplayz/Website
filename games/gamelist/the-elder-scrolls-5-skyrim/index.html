The Elder Scrolls 5: Skyrim

Overview

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim is a single-player action role-playing game from Bethesda Game Studios, directed by Todd Howard. It is the fifth main Elder Scrolls game, following Oblivion (2006), and is built on the Creation Engine. It was released in November 2011 and has been re-released many times since.

Key facts

  • Release dates: November 11, 2011 (Windows, PS3, Xbox 360); Legendary Edition June 4, 2013; Special Edition October 28, 2016 (PS4, Xbox One, Windows); Nintendo Switch November 17, 2017; Skyrim VR April 2, 2018; Anniversary Edition November 2021 (including PS5 and Xbox Series X/S); Nintendo Switch 2 December 9, 2025
  • Developer: Bethesda Game Studios
  • Publisher: Bethesda Softworks
  • Platforms: Windows, PlayStation 3, Xbox 360, PS4, Xbox One, PS5, Xbox Series X/S, Nintendo Switch, Nintendo Switch 2, VR headsets
  • Genre: Action role-playing, open world, single-player
  • Series: The Elder Scrolls (fifth main installment)

Gameplay

Players explore an open-world province inspired by Scandinavia in first or third person. There are ten playable races and 18 skills split across combat, magic and stealth. Players can dual-wield weapons and spells and learn dragon shouts by absorbing dragon souls. The main story can be ignored in favor of free exploration.

Story/Setting

The game is set 201 years after Oblivion in Skyrim, the northernmost province of Tamriel. The player is the "Last Dragonborn", who must defeat Alduin the World-Eater, a dragon prophesied to destroy the world. A civil war between the Imperial Legion and the Stormcloaks runs alongside the main quest.

Development

Planning began during development of Fallout 3, and full development started after its 2008 release. Roughly 100 developers worked on the game, which was designed by hand with 150 dungeons and 244 quests. The Creation Engine was forked from the Fallout 3 codebase. Jeremy Soule composed the music, and the main theme used a choir singing in the fictional dragon language. The voice cast has 70+ actors and over 60,000 lines of dialogue.

Reception

Metacritic scores listed on Wikipedia: PC 94, PS3 92, Xbox 360 96, PC Special Edition 74, PS4 81, Xbox One 82, Switch 84, Switch 2 60. Famitsu gave it 40/40, the first Western-developed game to earn that score. It was praised for character advancement, world design, visuals and dual-wielding, and criticized for melee combat, predictable dragon AI and launch-time technical issues. It won D.I.C.E. Game of the Year and Role-Playing Game of the Year, and was ranked No. 1 in PC Gamer's "The 100 Greatest PC Games of All Time".

Sales: 3.4 million physical units sold within two days of launch; 7 million shipped in the first week and 10 million by December 2011; over 20 million by June 2013; over 30 million by November 2016; over 60 million by June 2023; and 65 million by July 2026. Shipped units and sold units are different measures.

Legacy

Continued re-releases (Legendary, Special and Anniversary Editions, the latter with 74 Creation Club items) were credited by Bethesda to the "millions" still playing. Developers cite it as an influence on Valheim, The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and Avowed. It popularized large open-world fantasy RPGs and memes such as "Fus Ro Dah", and has an extensive modding community on Nexus Mods and Steam Workshop.
